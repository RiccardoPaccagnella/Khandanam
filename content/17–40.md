#### Translation

And, it is not the case that yours is not a fault in as much as the restriction in behaviour, which was made only voluntarily, results in accepting the existence of the means of valid knowledge etc,
because it is the knowledge of existence that results in there (in restriction of behaviour), not existence.
(The opponent might urge that after all the two positions are not so different,
because accepting the existence of the means of valid knowledge is the same as restricting behaviour)

Then, do you think that their existence should be accepted merely because we understand it to be so, or because our understanding of it is not sublated?

To start with, it cannot be the first,
because we would have to accept that the form of water of desert mirages etc truly exists.

And, in the second too, 
is it because only the arbiter, claimant and defendant do not have a sublated understanding (of their existence) only during the time of debate,
or is it because no one even at another time has a sublated cognition (of their existence)?

It's not the first,
because we would have an overextension:
As we often see that even something that is understood by three people at the same time might be sublated by another person – or or even by the same three people at another time.

And, it's not the case that that,
although that object does not exist,
despite its sublation it is still accepted as existent according to the apprehension of it that previously belonged to a handful of people.

Therefore, we are left with the second option.
That thing in which there is no sublation in any way at all should be accepted as existent.

#### Translation

For saying ‘We should behave according to this restriction’ means that the arbiter should understand ‘He (the claimant) has spoken according to this restriction.’

And, you should not say
‘Then you should accept the existence of his understanding too.’
For if we were to worry about its (the arbiter's understanding's) existence too,
we would have to resort precisely to another understanding of its (the arbiter's understanding's) existence.

And, it's not the case that in this way there is going to be an infinite regress,
because we do not acknowledge a necessity of following that (continuing the line of asking for a proof of the existence of the last element),
because of the maxim ‘In this way (since the absence of any fault is proved) the idea of something resulting from the knowledge of three or four is not superior.’ 
_There is no cognition that is better than the (certainty) which arises from three to four cognitions_

And, you shouldn't say:
‘If the last cognition does not exist,
all the previous cognitions won't exist by an undesirable domino effect.
And in this way you have not overcome the flaw even if you have accepted (the arbiter's) understanding.’

Let it be so,
because even so
we can mutually engage in a debate with assent
having struck an agreement
by an enquiry that is content by just looking for only up to the level of three or four cognitions
and, further, that is just enjoyable as it doesn't keep following through.
